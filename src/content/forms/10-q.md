A Form 10-Q gives investors an update between annual reports. It is shorter than a [10-K](/forms/10-k/) and focused on what changed during the quarter, but it follows the same logic: financial statements first, then management's explanation, then other required disclosures.

## When is a 10-Q due?

General Instruction A.1 of Form 10-Q sets the deadline by filer status:

| Filer status | Due after quarter end |
|---|---|
| Large accelerated filer | 40 days |
| Accelerated filer | 40 days |
| Non-accelerated filer | 45 days |

Under Exchange Act Rule 0-3, a deadline that falls on a Saturday, Sunday or federal holiday moves to the next business day. Large companies usually file well before the deadline, often on the same day as, or within a few days after, their earnings release. Our [due-date calendar](/calendar/) projects each company's next window from its own history.

If a company cannot file on time, it can submit Form 12b-25 (shown on EDGAR as NT 10-Q) no later than one business day after the due date, which gives it five more calendar days. See [what an NT filing means](/guides/nt-10-k-late-filing-notice/).

## What is inside a 10-Q?

**Part I, Financial Information**

- Item 1, Financial Statements: the balance sheet, income statement, cash flow statement and notes, unaudited but reviewed by the independent auditor.
- Item 2, Management's Discussion and Analysis: management's explanation of the quarter's results, liquidity and outlook factors.
- Item 3, Quantitative and Qualitative Disclosures About Market Risk.
- Item 4, Controls and Procedures: the conclusion of the chief executive and chief financial officer on disclosure controls.

**Part II, Other Information**

- Item 1, Legal Proceedings, and Item 1A, Risk Factors (material changes from the 10-K).
- Item 2, Unregistered Sales of Equity Securities and Use of Proceeds, which is where many companies report share repurchases made during the quarter.
- Items 3 to 5: defaults on senior securities, mine safety and other information. Item 5 is where companies disclose when a director or officer adopted or terminated a Rule 10b5-1 trading plan during the quarter.
- Item 6, Exhibits, including the Sarbanes-Oxley certifications.

## How should you read one?

Compare the quarter to the same quarter a year earlier, not just the previous quarter, because many businesses are seasonal. Read Item 2 next to the income statement: when revenue or margins move, MD&A is supposed to explain why. The notes often reveal new debt, litigation updates or segment changes that the earnings release mentioned only briefly.

Part II Item 1A is worth a quick scan. Companies are only required to report material changes to the risk factors in their 10-K, so a long new risk factor in a 10-Q is a signal that something has changed.

## 10-Q versus the earnings release

Most large companies publish an earnings press release first, furnished on a [Form 8-K](/forms/8-k/) under Item 2.02, and file the 10-Q afterwards. The press release often includes non-GAAP measures and management's chosen highlights. The 10-Q contains the complete GAAP financial statements and notes. When the two differ in emphasis, the 10-Q is the official record.
