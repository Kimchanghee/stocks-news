Form 144 is the **notice of proposed sale of securities** under Rule 144 of the Securities Act. It shows up in the EDGAR history of most large companies because officers and directors are affiliates, and their sales of company stock usually rely on Rule 144.

## How often does Form 144 appear on this site?

The stored EDGAR filing histories behind this site's 100 company pages (snapshot dated 20 Oct 2026) contain 274 Form 144 notices from 57 of the 100 companies. The histories cover different lengths of time for each company, and one seller can file many notices, so these are counts, not a measure of selling pressure.

## When is a Form 144 required?

An affiliate selling under Rule 144 must file the notice if the amount to be sold in any three-month period is more than 5,000 shares or units, or has an aggregate sale price above $50,000. The notice is filed at the time the sell order is placed with a broker, or the sale is executed directly with a market maker. Since 2023, Form 144 for securities of reporting companies must be filed electronically on EDGAR.

## Does a Form 144 mean the insider sold?

No. It states an intention and an approximate amount. The actual sale can be smaller, spread over time or not happen. For officers and directors, the completed transaction is reported on Form 4, generally within two business days. The form also asks whether the sale is made under a Rule 10b5-1 trading plan.

## How should you read one?

Check the seller's relationship to the issuer, the number of shares and approximate market value, the planned sale date and broker, and how the securities were acquired. Then look for the matching Form 4. This page is a plain-English reading of the rule and form, not investment advice; the SEC document is the authoritative record.
