Search EDGAR for almost any large company and you will find a steady rhythm of 8-K filings landing four times a year, a few weeks after each quarter ends. Most of them are earnings releases filed under **Item 2.02, Results of Operations and Financial Condition**. Knowing how this item works tells you what you can and cannot rely on when the numbers first appear.

## What triggers Item 2.02?

Item 2.02 applies when a company, or someone acting on its behalf, makes a public announcement or release disclosing material non-public information about its results of operations or financial condition for a **completed** quarterly or annual fiscal period. The usual trigger is the quarterly earnings press release.

The company must furnish the 8-K within **four business days** of that announcement. Large companies typically furnish it within minutes of the press release crossing the wire, because the release and the 8-K exhibit are the same document.

Two consequences follow from the wording:

- **Forecasts alone do not trigger Item 2.02.** Guidance about a future period is not about a completed period. Companies that update guidance between earnings dates often use Item 7.01 (Regulation FD Disclosure) or Item 8.01 (Other Events) instead.
- **The item is about the announcement, not the accounting.** Item 2.02 does not require a company to announce results at all. It requires a filing when the company chooses to make a public announcement of material results information.

## Where are the numbers?

The 8-K cover page will usually contain only a sentence or two under Item 2.02, saying that the company issued a press release and that the release is attached as an exhibit. Under **Item 9.01 Financial Statements and Exhibits** you will then see **Exhibit 99.1**, the press release itself. Some companies attach a second exhibit, often 99.2, holding a slide deck, a CFO commentary letter or supplemental tables.

When you open an earnings 8-K on EDGAR, skip straight to the filing index and open the 99.1 document. That is where you find revenue, earnings per share, segment tables, any guidance and the reconciliations discussed below.

## What does "furnished, not filed" mean?

General Instruction B.2 of Form 8-K says information furnished under Item 2.02 (and Item 7.01) is not deemed "filed" for purposes of Section 18 of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 and is not incorporated by reference into registration statements or other filings, unless the company expressly says so.

In plain terms:

- Section 18 creates liability for false or misleading statements in documents *filed* with the SEC. Furnished information sits outside that particular provision.
- Furnished does **not** mean unaccountable. The general antifraud provisions, including Rule 10b-5, still apply to what the company says.
- Furnished information is not automatically pulled into a later securities offering document. A company that wants it there has to say so.

You will often see a sentence in the 8-K confirming this treatment. If a company instead elects to "file" the information, the 8-K will say that too.

## How do non-GAAP rules apply to earnings releases?

Most earnings releases show "adjusted" figures, such as adjusted EPS, adjusted EBITDA or free cash flow, that are not defined by U.S. GAAP. Two sets of rules govern these measures:

1. **Regulation G** applies whenever a public company publicly discloses a non-GAAP financial measure. It requires presentation of the most directly comparable GAAP measure and a quantitative reconciliation between the two, and it prohibits non-GAAP measures that are misleading.
2. **Item 10(e) of Regulation S-K** sets more detailed requirements for filings. Instruction 2 to Item 2.02 applies Item 10(e)(1)(i) to earnings information furnished under Item 2.02, so the press release must present the comparable GAAP measure **with equal or greater prominence**, provide a reconciliation, and explain why management believes the non-GAAP measure is useful.

The SEC staff's Non-GAAP Compliance and Disclosure Interpretations give examples of measures and presentations the staff views as potentially misleading, such as excluding normal, recurring cash operating expenses. When you read an earnings release, look for the reconciliation tables, usually near the end of Exhibit 99.1, and compare the GAAP and adjusted lines line by line.

## What about the earnings call?

The conference call that follows an earnings release can also disclose material results information orally. Item 2.02 includes a conditional exemption: an oral, telephonic or webcast announcement does not need its own 8-K if it occurs within 48 hours of a related written release that was furnished under Item 2.02, is broadly accessible to the public, was announced in advance with access details, and any non-GAAP information discussed is covered by Regulation G reconciliation made available on the company's website. The precise conditions are in the Form 8-K instructions linked below.

That is why you rarely see a separate 8-K for the call itself, although some companies voluntarily furnish the transcript or slides under Item 7.01.

## How does the earnings 8-K relate to the 10-Q and 10-K?

The sequence for a typical large accelerated filer looks like this:

1. **Quarter ends.**
2. **Earnings release + Item 2.02 8-K**, usually three to six weeks later.
3. **10-Q (or 10-K)**, filed by the deadline: 40 days after quarter end for large accelerated and accelerated filers, 45 days for others; 60, 75 or 90 days after year end for the 10-K depending on filer status.

The 10-Q or 10-K is the complete, filed report, with notes, risk factors and management's discussion and analysis. Numbers in the earnings release can be revised before the periodic report is filed; when that happens, the periodic report is the version that counts. ## Reading an earnings 8-K in practice

A short checklist:

- **Confirm the item.** The cover should list Item 2.02 and Item 9.01. If you see Item 4.02 (non-reliance on previously issued financial statements) alongside it, slow down; that is a restatement signal.
- **Note the period.** The release should name the quarter or year and the period end date.
- **Find GAAP first.** Read GAAP revenue, operating income, net income and diluted EPS before any adjusted figure.
- **Read the reconciliation.** List the adjustments and ask whether they recur every quarter.
- **Check guidance language.** Forward-looking statements are accompanied by cautionary language; guidance is a projection, not a reported result.
- **Wait for the 10-Q.** Segment detail, legal contingencies and cash-flow notes appear in the periodic report.
