Most large U.S. companies file their 10-Q and 10-K reports days before the deadline. When one cannot, it files a short notice on **Form 12b-25**, which appears on EDGAR as **NT 10-K** or **NT 10-Q**. These notices are rare among the largest companies, which is exactly why they are worth reading carefully when they appear.

## How does Rule 12b-25 work?

Rule 12b-25 under the Securities Exchange Act lets a company that cannot file all or part of a periodic report on time obtain a short extension, if it meets three conditions:

1. It files Form 12b-25 **no later than one business day after** the report's original due date.
2. It states in the notice that the reasons the report could not be filed on time **could not be eliminated without unreasonable effort or expense**.
3. It files the report within the extension period.

If those conditions are met, the late report is **deemed timely filed**.

| Report | Normal deadline (large accelerated filer) | Extension under 12b-25 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10-K | 60 days after fiscal year end | 15 calendar days |
| 10-Q | 40 days after quarter end | 5 calendar days |

Deadlines differ by filer status: the 10-K is due 60, 75 or 90 days after year end, and the 10-Q 40 or 45 days after quarter end, depending on whether the company is a large accelerated, accelerated or non-accelerated filer. Our [deadline guide](/guides/10-q-10-k-filing-deadlines/) covers the details, including how weekends and federal holidays roll a due date forward.

## What is inside the notice?

Form 12b-25 is short, but each part tells you something:

- **Part I, Registrant information.** Name and address.
- **Part II, Rules 12b-25(b) and (c).** Check boxes confirming the conditions above. If an accountant's statement is required, because the auditor's work is part of the reason for the delay, it is attached as an exhibit.
- **Part III, Narrative.** The company must describe in reasonable detail why the report cannot be filed on time. This is the most informative section.
- **Part IV, Other information.** The company answers whether all other required periodic reports have been filed and whether it anticipates any **significant change in results of operations** from the corresponding period of the last fiscal year that will be reflected in the report. If yes, it must explain the anticipated change and, if appropriate, give a reasonable estimate.

## How should you read the reason?

The narrative in Part III ranges from routine to alarming. Some common categories:

- **Administrative or system delays,** for example completing work on a recent acquisition or a new accounting system.
- **Pending accounting questions,** such as evaluating the treatment of a complex transaction.
- **Internal control or audit issues,** such as the auditor needing more time or management identifying a material weakness.
- **Restatement or investigation,** for example an audit committee review. These often come with an 8-K under **Item 4.02** (non-reliance on previously issued financial statements).

Read the Part IV answer about significant changes alongside the narrative. A company that expects to report results materially different from last year is required to say so, even before the report is filed.

## What if the extension is missed?

If the report is not filed within the 12b-25 extension period, it is delinquent. Several practical consequences can follow:

- **Form S-3 eligibility.** Short-form shelf registration generally requires timely filing of Exchange Act reports over the prior 12 months. A report filed late, beyond the 12b-25 window, can cost the company this eligibility for a year.
- **Exchange listing rules.** Both major U.S. exchanges have rules for listed companies that fail to file periodic reports, typically starting with a notice and a compliance period. Such a notice often triggers an 8-K under Item 3.01.
- **Investor attention.** Delays tend to coincide with the other filings listed above.

## How common are NT filings among large companies?

They are uncommon. Large accelerated filers have the resources to meet the 40- and 60-day deadlines, and most file well ahead of them, which you can see in the filing-lag charts on each [company page](/companies/). That is why an NT 10-K or NT 10-Q from a large company is unusual enough to read in full, along with any 8-K filed the same week.
